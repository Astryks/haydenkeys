Privacy Policy

Last updated 2026-10-05. Plain language, and accurate to exactly what this app does — not boilerplate.

The short version

Hayden Keys has no account system, no server, and no database. It's a static web app that runs entirely in your browser. There is nothing to sign up for, nothing we store about you on a server, and nothing we sell — we have no server to store it on or business selling it from.

What's stored, and where

Hayden Keys remembers your lesson progress, practice streak, earned badges, saved/completed songs, and keyboard calibration settings using your browser's own local storage — a standard browser feature, private to your device. This data:

  • Never leaves your device or gets sent to us or anyone else.
  • Isn't tied to your identity in any way — there's no account, no email, no name attached to it.
  • Can be erased at any time by clearing your browser's site data for haydenkeys.com — or, in the iPhone/iPad app, by deleting the app.

Camera access

The Practice tab's Camera Overlay mode can use your device's camera to show you which key to press next, overlaid live on a view of your real keyboard. The video feed is used only live, in your browser, to calculate where to draw that overlay — it is never recorded, saved, uploaded, or transmitted anywhere. You can decline camera access and still use every other part of the app; Camera Overlay just won't be available.

Microphone access

Hayden Keys asks for microphone access in three places, all entirely on-device:

  • Keyboard calibration ("Get Started") — listens briefly to confirm which physical key you played, by ear, using real pitch-detection math running in your browser.
  • Ear Check practice mode — listens to check you played the right note while practicing a song.
  • "Tune this note" buttons in lessons — a tuner-style needle showing whether the note you played on your real piano matches the one on screen.

In every case, audio is analyzed live and never recorded, stored, or sent anywhere, and the microphone only turns on when you tap to start one of these. You can decline microphone access and still use the rest of the app; those features just won't work.

MIDI keyboards

If you tap "Connect MIDI keyboard" (supported in Chrome and Edge on a computer), your browser asks permission to read which keys you press on a connected keyboard. Those key presses are used only live, on your device, to check what you played — never recorded, stored, or sent anywhere.

Uploading your own recording

If you upload an audio/video file to have Hayden Keys figure out its notes, that file is processed entirely in your browser using an on-device machine learning model (Spotify's open-source basic-pitch, vendored directly in this app's own code — see THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md). The file is never uploaded to a server; it never leaves your device.

The only third-party network request, disclosed honestly

The Discover tab looks up each song's album cover art using Apple's free, public iTunes Search API — sending just a song title and artist name as a search query, nothing about you personally. The cover images themselves then load from Apple's image servers. This is a lookup, not a data collection mechanism, and no response is stored beyond your current session.

The real piano sound comes from public-domain Steinway samples that are bundled with the app itself — loading them never contacts any third-party server.

"Which song is this?" (iPhone and iPad app)

When you upload a song in the iPhone/iPad app, the app may try to name it using Apple's ShazamKit. It turns about 12 seconds of the recording into an audio fingerprint on your device and sends only that fingerprint to Apple to look the song up, never the recording itself. We don't receive or store it. Apple's privacy policy applies to that lookup. The website doesn't do this.

Optional videos

A few "Did you know?" facts have a Watch button for a short video. Nothing is loaded from YouTube until you tap it; on the website it then plays from YouTube's privacy-enhanced domain (youtube-nocookie.com), and in the iPhone/iPad app it opens in YouTube. YouTube's own privacy policy applies to what you watch there. Photos of musicians are bundled with the app (freely licensed, credited under each photo) and make no network request.

What we don't do

  • No account creation, no login, no password.
  • No analytics, no trackers, no advertising, no third-party ad SDKs.
  • No selling, renting, or sharing of any data — we have none to share.
  • No cookies used for tracking (none at all, beyond what your browser itself may use for local storage).

Children's privacy

Hayden Keys collects no personal information from anyone, of any age — there's no account system or data collection to begin with, so this holds equally whether you're 8 or 80.

Changes to this policy

If this app's real behavior changes in a way that affects this policy, this page will be updated to match — same honesty standard as the rest of the project (see STATUS.md for a full, dated history of what's actually been built).
